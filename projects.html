01 · Business Intelligence

Superstore Sales Performance

visualization
SQLTableauEDARetail Analytics

Conducted an end-to-end analysis of four years of retail sales data for a fictional office supply company, surfacing regional performance gaps, product-level profitability drivers, and seasonal trends.

Overview

The dataset contained order-level transactional records across three product categories — Furniture, Office Supplies, and Technology — spanning multiple US regions. The goal was to move from raw data to a clear strategic recommendation about where the business should invest and where it should cut.

Key Findings
  • The West region drove the highest revenue but the Central region consistently underperformed on margin.
  • Technology was the highest-margin category; Furniture (particularly Tables) operated at a loss after discounts.
  • Heavy discounting in the Central region correlated directly with negative profit — reducing discounts by 10% would recover an estimated $18k annually.
  • Q4 accounted for ~35% of annual revenue, suggesting strong seasonal concentration risk.
Methodology

Data was cleaned and aggregated in SQL (DuckDB), then visualized in Tableau. Profit margin was decomposed by region × category × sub-category to isolate loss drivers. A discount sensitivity analysis was run to quantify margin impact.

03 · Operations Research

COTA Bus Stop Optimization

optimization
PythonGurobiMCLPGISNetwork Analysis

Applied the Maximal Covering Location Problem (MCLP) to identify 5 optimal new bus stop locations across Columbus, Ohio — maximizing the number of residents brought within walking distance of the COTA network under a fixed infrastructure budget.

Formulation

The MCLP selects P facility locations from a candidate set C to maximize the total demand covered within a service radius S. A demand point is "covered" if at least one selected facility falls within distance S of it.

$$ \begin{aligned} &\text{max } \sum_{i \in D} h_i y_i \\ &\text{subject to:} \\ &\quad \sum_{j \in N_i} x_j \geq y_i \quad \forall i \in D \\ &\quad \sum_{j \in C} x_j = P \\ &\quad x_j \in \{0,1\} \quad \forall j \in C \\ &\quad y_i \in \{0,1\} \quad \forall i \in D \\ &\text{where:} \\ &\quad h_i = \text{population at demand point } i \\ &\quad N_i = \{j \in C : d_{ij} \leq S\} \\ &\quad P = \text{number of stops to place (5)} \\ &\quad C = \text{candidate stop locations} \\ &\quad D = \text{demand points (census blocks)} \end{aligned} $$
Results

Grey dots represent existing stops, blue dots show candidate locations, and red dots mark the 5 optimal selected stops. The model adds 42,986 additional residents to covered population.

04 · Energy Analytics

IGS Energy — Battery Optimization

optimization
RTime-Series RegressionEnergyCost Savings

Developed a time-series regression model on energy consumption data to optimize residential battery dispatch — projecting $1,700+ in annual customer savings by charging during off-peak hours and discharging during peak pricing windows.

Time-Series Price Model
$$ \begin{aligned} &\log(\text{Price}_t) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \log(\text{Price}_{t-1}) + \beta_2 \log(\text{DayOfWeek}_{t-24}) + \beta_3 \text{Weekend}_t + \\ &\beta_4 \text{SevereWeather}_t + \beta_5 \text{MaxTemp}_t + \beta_6 \text{HeatPeak}_t + \beta_7 \text{HeatNonPeak}_t + \epsilon_t \end{aligned} $$
Dispatch Optimization
$$ \begin{aligned} &\text{min } \sum_i [\alpha_i \cdot \beta_i + c_i - d_i], \quad i = 1, \ldots, 24 \\ &\text{s.t. } d_i + \text{grid}_i \leq 13.5\text{ kW}, \quad c_i + d_i \leq 13.5\text{ kW}, \quad c_i \leq 5\text{ kW}, \quad d_i \leq 11.5\text{ kW} \end{aligned} $$
Assumptions
  • Battery cannot be charged and discharged simultaneously.
  • No more than two batteries are fully cycled per day.
  • Initial state of charge: 0 kWh.
Due to data confidentiality, detailed results cannot be shared publicly. The model produced a full 24-hour dispatch schedule and achieved significant verified cost savings.
05 · Probabilistic Modeling

Stock Market Prediction — Bayesian Hierarchical Model

bayesian
RStanBayesian InferenceHierarchical ModelFinance

Built a Bayesian hierarchical model to estimate company- and sector-level probabilities of positive stock returns. Because companies within a sector share economic influences, the model uses partial pooling to borrow information across companies — producing more stable, uncertainty-aware estimates than independent models.

Model Specification
$$ \begin{aligned} y_{scf} &\overset{ind}{\sim} \text{Bernoulli}(\theta_{sc}) \\ \text{logit}(\theta_{sc}) = \eta_{sc} &\mid \mu_s, \tau_s \sim N(\mu_s,\, \tau_s^{-1}) \\ \mu_s &\sim N(0,\, 20) \\ \tau_s &\sim \text{Gamma}(1,\, 0.1) \end{aligned} $$
Parameter Interpretations
  • \(y_{scf}\): 1 if company \(c\) in sector \(s\) had a positive return at observation \(f\), else 0.
  • \(\theta_{sc}\): Probability of a positive return for company \(c\) in sector \(s\).
  • \(\mu_s\): Sector-level mean log-odds of a positive return.
  • \(\tau_s\): Precision (inverse variance) within sector \(s\) — higher = more consistent.
Key Findings

Sectors 1, 2, and 5 are recommended for investment. Company 10 in sector 2 and company 5 in sector 5 showed the highest posterior probabilities of positive returns. Sector 1 exhibited the highest posterior precision — indicating the most consistent company performance and lowest within-sector variance, making it the most stable choice.
